# Auditable reputation network (WS-H)

This is a disabled-by-default shadow pilot, not a blacklist or automatic dispatch rule. Outcomes may reflect courier problems, mistaken records or reassigned/shared phones. A return or refusal is not proof of buyer fraud. The model, queue decisions, and WhatsApp eligibility do not read these counts.

## Consent and contribution

- The proposed pilot basis is separate, affirmative buyer permission for the named seller to share courier outcome counts with participating sellers. It is optional, expires after 90 days and does not affect checkout, fulfillment or WhatsApp consent. The seller must explain the purpose, recipients, retention, how to withdraw and how to appeal. Do not treat a checkout number or an existing WhatsApp opt-in as network consent. Before pilot activation, assign a privacy steward, publish a staffed contact channel in every seller's notice, confirm contracts and local obligations, and agree how to verify consent and audit misuse. This document is not a compliance determination.
- The host allowlists participating tenant IDs via `COD_GUARD_NETWORK_TENANTS` (comma separated, excludes `demo`) and provisions a unique random secret via `COD_GUARD_NETWORK_KEY` (32 random bytes or more, URL-safe Base64 without padding). Each seller separately enrolls using `PUT /network/enrollment` with `enabled: true`. Enable only merchants who have accepted the pilot terms and have a real consent workflow. Store the key separately from database and backups. A mismatched key disables matching; plan migrations with the steward so suppression records are not lost.
- For an eligible order, `PUT /network/orders/{order_id}/consent` with `enabled: true` and a non-PII `evidence_reference` records the operator's attestation. Sellers must record fresh consent before new courier outcomes and not reimport historical shipments as new observations. Only the first terminal courier label recorded after the consent contributes, once per order. Manual labels, synthetic/demo rows and Daraz do not contribute. Corrections remove prior contributions. Operator CSVs are seller-attested, not independently validated by this code; the steward should sample evidence and suspend abusive sellers.

## What another seller sees

`GET /network/orders/{order_id}/counts` requires an order in the caller's own tenant and separate active consent for that phone. It returns only aggregate delivered, returned and refused counts, and only when at least three distinct **other** sellers contribute for that phone. There is no arbitrary phone lookup, and absent/suppressed/interested-but-insufficient data all receive `counts: null`. No other seller name, order ID, timestamp, items, fees, address, raw phone or digest is shared. Orders remain in their own tenant records; network tables use a purpose-separated HMAC-SHA256 of the normalized phone and internal order references for deduplication and correction. Digests are **pseudonymous**, not anonymous; treat the database and key as sensitive. See [ICO technical guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation/) for hashing considerations, not a statement of local law.

`GET /network/size` and the dashboard show the number of distinct currently allowlisted sellers with at least one unexpired contribution. Neither enrolled-but-empty sellers nor synthetic fixtures count. Disabled and zero are shown distinctly. This measures participation, not customers, market reach, or merchant pilots.

## Withdrawal, correction and removal

1. The buyer can ask their seller to withdraw or dispute the history via the contact published by that seller. Verify control of the phone and connection to the order privately, issue a case reference, and record the request outside the network. Do not request identity documents or phone numbers in a public issue. The seller can withdraw locally on the same consent endpoint with `enabled: false` or use the order outcome correction if a label is wrong. Seller withdrawal (`PUT /network/enrollment`, `enabled: false`) removes that seller's network data.
2. For network-wide withdrawal or disputed attribution, the steward verifies the case and runs `python scripts/network_maintenance.py --remove-subject CASE_REFERENCE` from a private host terminal. The phone is entered without echo. This deletes all matching consent/contribution records and blocks silent re-addition using a keyed suppression entry; the network audit stores only the action and opaque case reference. Default to suppression during a credible attribution dispute; communicate the decision and available recourse privately. v1 does not automatically lift suppressions or infer whether a phone has changed owners.
3. Expired data is excluded on reads; run `python scripts/network_maintenance.py --purge-expired` daily. Keep backup retention aligned; do not restore a backup into service until withdrawals and removals since it was taken have been reapplied. Subject removal here affects only network data, not the seller's underlying order or carrier records. Review how long to keep suppressions before onboarding and keep them only for their limited prevention purpose.

No real buyers, merchant agreements, appeals staff, or network growth are assumed or validated by these fixture tests.
